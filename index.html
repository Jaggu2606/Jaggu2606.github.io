Robotics Projects

AdaTerrainDiff: Terrain-Conditioned Adaptive Action Refinement for Unified Bipedal Locomotion

Ongoing work · 2nd-generation biped · IIT Mandi

Diffusion-based locomotion controllers such as BiRoDiff and DiffuseLoco use one mixed latent and a fixed number of denoising steps, so they treat every terrain the same. AdaTerrainDiff splits the encoder into a 128-D state latent and a 16-D terrain latent built from the height map. The terrain latent then sets the action generation: 5 denoising steps on flat ground and up to 10 on stairs, with stochasticity scaled to the terrain. An x0-prediction denoiser stays stable with only a few steps, and the policy is trained with online PPO.

One policy walks on flat ground, slopes, rough terrain, discrete obstacles and stairs. Averaged over all terrains it reaches 81.0% success, versus 78.0% for BiRoDiff and 32.1% for DiffuseLoco. The biggest gains are on discrete terrain (+12 points) and steep slopes (+5.2 points). On the 10-DOF hardware biped, sim-to-real trials completed 98.3% of each 60-second episode, with cost of transport within 13% of simulation.

Terrain-Adaptive Locomotion for a 10-DOF Biped with Shared Autonomy

2nd-generation biped · IIT Mandi

Industrial inspection sites such as manufacturing floors, power plants and refineries have ramps, grated flooring and irregular ground, and people work close by. Most humanoid locomotion work is validated on idealized flat surfaces. This project builds a 10-DOF biped, based on the Berkeley Humanoid Lite URDF, that is meant for those environments.

A reinforcement learning architecture with structured terrain encoding gives stable walking on flat ground, stair ascent and descent, and uneven terrain. Policies are trained in physics simulation with domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer. A low-level safety control loop enforces stability margins and joint-level compliance, so the robot stays predictable and interruptible around human inspectors. The hardware work included custom BLDC actuator control and PID tuning, a range-of-motion study against human joint limits, and gantry-supported sim-to-real trials, including push-recovery tests where the robot is shoved mid-stance and has to regain balance.

Multi-Skill Bipedal Robot with Learning-Based Control

Mar 2024 – Sep 2026 · M.Tech thesis, IIT Mandi

Designed a small-scale biped robot from the ground up, covering mechanical design, kinematic modeling and stability analysis. Trained locomotion policies with PPO, TD3 and SAC that achieve robust walking and running at over 90% success, using waypoint-based reward shaping to learn multiple skills on an underactuated platform.

Policies were developed in Gazebo, PyBullet and Isaac Gym and deployed on hardware through a ROS2 sim-to-real pipeline.

Hierarchical RL for Collision-Free Locomotion of an Underactuated Biped

Feb 2026 – Jul 2026

A two-level control architecture for obstacle-aware bipedal locomotion. A high-level waypoint policy plans a path through the obstacles, and a low-level gait policy turns it into stable footsteps. The policy was trained in PyBullet with up to 20 obstacles, then deployed on the 1st-generation biped.

In simulation it reaches 98% success with path efficiency (SPL) of 0.955. On hardware it keeps 92% success and SPL 0.884, a sim-to-real gap of about 6–7%.

Deep RL for Vision-Based UAV Tracking by an Autonomous Ground Vehicle

Jan 2026 – Aug 2026 · Centre for AI & Robotics, IIT Mandi

Persistent tracking of a DJI Matrice 600 UAV by a ground vehicle (Diadem AGV) using deep reinforcement learning, with body-frame velocity commands as actions. A YOLOv8n detector, trained on a custom dataset of 5,000 annotated frames, runs in real time on an upward-facing camera. The centroid of the detected bounding box gives the planar tracking error on the image plane.

A composite reward adds a bounded exponential proximity kernel, a potential-based progress term that is guaranteed not to change the optimal policy, and an action-smoothness penalty. Five algorithms (DQN, TD3, SAC, TQC and TD7) were benchmarked on circular and square trajectories. TD7 reached the lowest steady-state error and converged fastest, and an ablation showed that the reward shaping speeds up convergence for every algorithm.

Autonomous Buried Power Cable Tracing

Research Associate work · IIT Mandi

A ground robot that follows buried power cables using electromagnetic locator feedback. It steers to keep the signal strong, avoids obstacles without a predefined goal, and builds a GPS map of the cable route as it drives. The video shows the ROS/Gazebo simulation of the AGV tracing a buried cable path. This is part of my work as a Research Associate at IIT Mandi.
